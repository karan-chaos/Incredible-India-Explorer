The Spice Route

How Indian Spices Changed the World

For millennia, Indian spices flowed westward through the Spice Route — a network of ancient sea and land routes connecting the Malabar Coast to Rome, Arabia, Southeast Asia, and China. Pepper was so valuable it was called "black gold." The quest for direct access to India's spices launched the Age of Exploration, led Vasco da Gama to Calicut in 1498, and ultimately shaped the modern world map.
