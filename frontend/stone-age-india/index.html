MAJOR PERIODS

Three stages of Stone Age life

01 Old Stone Age

Paleolithic

c. 2 million BCE – 10,000 BCE

Early communities depended mainly on hunting, gathering and wild resources. Large stone tools such as handaxes and cleavers became important technologies.

  • Hunter-gatherer communities
  • Handaxes and cleavers
  • Rock shelters and open-air sites
02 Middle Stone Age

Mesolithic

c. 10,000 – 6,000 BCE

Smaller microlithic tools became widespread as people adapted to changing post-Ice Age environments. Hunting, fishing and gathering remained important.

  • Microliths
  • Rock art
  • Burials and seasonal settlements
03 New Stone Age

Neolithic

c. 7,000 – 2,000 BCE

Farming, animal domestication, pottery and more permanent settlements became increasingly important, although the transition occurred at different times across India.

  • Ground and polished tools
  • Agriculture and herding
  • Settled village communities

STONE TECHNOLOGY

Tools that shaped prehistoric life

🪓

Handaxes

Bifacially worked tools associated especially with Acheulean technology. They were useful for cutting, butchering and processing materials.

🔨

Cleavers

Large cutting tools with broad working edges that form part of the Acheulean toolkit found at several Indian Paleolithic sites.

🔹

Microliths

Small geometric and blade-like stone tools became characteristic of many Mesolithic assemblages and could be used as composite tool components.

⚒️

Ground Stone Tools

Polished axes and related tools became increasingly important during the Neolithic, particularly for woodworking and farming activities.

TIMELINE

A long prehistoric journey

~2 million BCE

Early Paleolithic traditions

Early stone-tool technologies appear in the Indian subcontinent.

~1.5 million BCE

Acheulean expansion

Acheulean handaxes and cleavers become an important part of the archaeological record at sites such as Attirampakkam.

~10,000 BCE

Mesolithic transition

Microlithic technologies become widespread as communities adapt to post-Ice Age environments.

~7,000 BCE

Early Neolithic communities

Farming, animal management and more permanent settlements emerge in different parts of the subcontinent at different times.

~3,000–2,000 BCE

Established village traditions

Neolithic lifeways become established across several regional traditions before later Chalcolithic developments.
