01 · Origin

Born in the High Himalaya

🏔️

Himalayan Origin

The Teesta system begins in the high Himalayan landscape of North Sikkim, with the Teesta Khangtse glacier and streams associated with Tso Lhamo and the surrounding glacial lakes feeding its upper course.

💧

Chungthang Confluence

The river takes the name Teesta after the Lachan Chu and Lachung Chu meet at Chungthang in North Sikkim.

🗺️

Two Indian States

Its dramatic mountain course crosses Sikkim before the river continues through northern West Bengal and onward into Bangladesh.

02 · Meaning & History

From Tri-Srota to Teesta

Teesta is also commonly written as Tista. Historical descriptions associate the river with the Sanskrit name Tri-Srota, meaning "three streams", reflecting the river's historically divided channels.

Older descriptions of northern Bengal record channels such as Mara Tista, Bura Tista and Dadai. Major floods and changes in river channels altered the course over time.

Today, the Teesta remains one of the defining river systems of the eastern Himalaya, linking mountain landscapes with the plains.

04 · Glacial Landscape

A River Shaped by Ice and Altitude

❄️ Glaciers

Snow and glacier-fed streams supply the upper Teesta basin. High-altitude lakes and glaciers make the source region one of Sikkim's most striking landscapes.

⛰️ Deep Gorges

From the high mountains, the river drops rapidly through steep valleys and narrow gorges, creating powerful currents and a large elevation gradient.

🌊 Changing Channels

When the river reaches the plains, its gradient decreases and its channels become wider and more dynamic, especially during the monsoon.

05 · People & Livelihoods

Water for Farms and Communities

🌾

Agriculture

Teesta water supports agriculture across the river basin. Rice, maize, vegetables and other crops benefit from irrigation, while the surrounding Sikkim hills are also known for high-value crops such as large cardamom.

🍃

North Bengal

In West Bengal, the river system supports irrigation in northern districts and forms an important part of the region's water landscape.

⚡

Hydropower

The steep elevation drop in Sikkim has made the Teesta basin important for hydropower development, including projects such as Teesta Stage III and Teesta Stage V.
